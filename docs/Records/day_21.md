# Day 21

The writing workflow is where research either comes together—or falls apart.

In ScholarOS, a conversation can use assembled context from recent messages, saved memory, and retrieved project knowledge. The context assembler caps recent history at 10 messages and 4,000 characters, so the prompt doesn’t grow without bound as a conversation gets longer.

That’s a technical constraint with a writing consequence. If the context window is filled with every past message, there’s less room for the current question and relevant source material. If too little context is included, the assistant may lose the thread.

ScholarOS also creates a rolling summary after enough new conversation messages accumulate, then uses that summary to carry older context forward. It’s a practical continuity mechanism, but summaries are compressed: they can’t preserve every detail, so retrieved source evidence still matters.

I’m working to make that continuity useful while keeping the sources visible and the writer in control.

What part of moving from research to a first draft is hardest for you: deciding what to include, organizing the argument, or keeping track of the evidence?
