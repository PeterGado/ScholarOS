# Day 3

One thing I've realized while building ScholarOS is that this is not a product for people who just use AI.

It's for people whose work depends on understanding information, not just collecting it.

I keep thinking about researchers, students, writers, analysts, and knowledge workers: people who read widely, compare ideas, and have to turn what they learn into something useful. Many of us still bounce between notes, PDFs, browser tabs, and drafts just to keep our thinking together.

The problem isn’t that we don’t have enough information. It’s that the work around that information is fragmented.

Notes live in one tool. Sources live in another. Drafts live somewhere else. The connections between an idea, the evidence behind it, and the decision to use it often stay in a person’s head instead of in the system they’re working with.

That’s the kind of work I want ScholarOS to support—not simply storing more material, but helping people keep the context they need as they move from reading to thinking to writing.

Whether you’re reading deeply, building an argument, writing from evidence, or trying to make sense of a growing body of material, context matters just as much as the final output.

That’s the product I’m trying to build, and I know the details will depend on the people doing this work every day.

If your work depends on research, writing, or turning complex information into something useful, I’d love to hear how you keep it all together. What tools or habits help you hold on to context?
