# Day 3

One thing I've realized while building ScholarOS is that this is not a product for people who just use AI.

It's for people whose work depends on understanding information, not just collecting it.

I keep thinking about researchers, students, writers, analysts, and knowledge workers. People who read a lot, synthesize a lot, and still end up bouncing between notes, PDFs, and drafts just to keep their thinking together.

The problem isn't that they don't have enough information. It's that the work is fragmented.

Notes live in one tool. Sources live in another. Drafts live somewhere else. The important connections between ideas, evidence, and decisions stay in a person's head instead of in the system they're using.

That's the kind of work ScholarOS is meant to support.

Whether you're reading deeply, building arguments, writing from evidence, or trying to make sense of a growing body of material, context matters just as much as output.

That's the product I'm trying to build.

If your work depends on research, writing, or turning complex information into something useful, I'd love to hear how you keep it all together.
