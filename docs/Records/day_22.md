# Day 22

Writing is rarely a straight line from first draft to final version.

One product detail I’ve learned to be careful about is describing features that aren’t there. ScholarOS currently has conversations, writing profiles, and named writing segments; it does not have the old draft-review pipeline or full document version history.

That distinction matters. It’s tempting to talk about the complete writing lifecycle as though every stage is implemented, but an honest product description should separate what exists from what remains an idea.

The current writing work is focused on conversation context, persistent memory, and user-specific style/profile information. Revision history and review workflows are separate product problems that need their own design and implementation.

When you revise a piece of writing, what do you most need to keep track of: the changes themselves, feedback, source evidence, or the reasoning behind decisions?
