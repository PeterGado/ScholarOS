# Day 12

Many knowledge tools are great at one of two things: storing information or generating something new.

I’m interested in the space between them. In ScholarOS, extracted content is divided into knowledge chunks, and each chunk can retain an evidence link to its source document. That makes a chunk a retrieval unit with provenance, rather than an anonymous piece of text.

Saving a document doesn’t guarantee you’ll understand or use it later. Generating text doesn’t guarantee it reflects the material you’ve collected. The system needs an indexing and retrieval layer that can bring useful context back while preserving where it came from.

That layer—understanding, retrieval, and continuity—is a big part of what I’m building with ScholarOS. It’s less visible than a file upload or a generated paragraph, but it may be what turns a collection of features into a useful workflow.

What feels most missing from the tools you use: better organization, better search, or better ways to use what you’ve saved? If you had to choose one improvement, which would save you the most time?
