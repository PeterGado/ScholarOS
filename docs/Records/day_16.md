# Day 16

An AI answer can sound convincing and still be unhelpful if you can’t see what it’s based on.

In ScholarOS, retrieved knowledge chunks carry evidence links back to research documents. That means the retrieval result isn’t meant to be a floating paragraph: the system can include the source document details alongside the passage it found.

For research and knowledge work, that connection matters. If a tool summarizes a point or suggests a conclusion, you need to check where it came from, decide whether the source is relevant, and bring your own judgment to it.

Without source provenance, a fluent answer can create extra work: you have to verify every detail from scratch, or risk relying on something you can’t evaluate. Evidence links don’t guarantee an answer is correct, but they make it possible to inspect the material and decide for yourself.

That’s why I think grounding is a product concern, not just a technical detail. What would help you feel confident using an AI-supported answer: source links, relevant excerpts, a clear explanation of uncertainty, or something else?
