# Day 11

Building a product has reminded me that a good technical idea is not enough.

You also have to understand where the work actually happens: how people gather sources, take notes, make sense of what they’ve read, and turn it into something they can share. Each person has a slightly different process, and the messy parts are often hidden behind habits they’ve developed over time.

That’s one reason I’m building ScholarOS as a modular monolith rather than starting with microservices. The backend has separate modules for documents, knowledge, projects, and writing, with clear interfaces between them—but it can still be developed and deployed as one application.

It’s a pragmatic engineering choice: preserve boundaries in the code without taking on distributed-system complexity before there’s a demonstrated need. But architecture alone can’t tell me whether the workflow fits people’s lives.

Where does your current research or writing workflow feel smooth—and where does it break down? I’d be interested in hearing about both sides.
